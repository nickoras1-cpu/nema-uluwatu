How to Choose a Bali Retreat Venue: A Checklist

Twelve things to check before you sign, from rain backup to room configuration.

By the Nema. Uluwatu team · 23 October 2026 · 6 min read

The venue is the single biggest decision in planning a retreat. It sets your price, your participants' experience and how much you will be firefighting on the day. Here is the checklist we would use, from the other side of the reception desk.

The checklist

  1. Exclusive use

    Can you reserve the whole property? Sharing a venue with other guests changes the energy of a retreat completely, and makes quiet practice and group moments harder.

  2. Room configuration

    Count beds, not rooms. Decide whether participants get private rooms or share, because that drives your pricing. Twelve rooms with one queen bed each, for example, suits 12 people in private rooms or up to 24 sharing.

  3. Practice space

    Where will you actually teach? Look at the surface, shade, noise and how many mats it fits. Ask for photos taken at the time of day you plan to teach.

  4. Rain backup

    Bali's wet season runs roughly from November to March. A covered area for practice is non-negotiable if your dates fall in those months.

  5. Meals

    Is catering included, optional or not offered? If not offered, are there good cafes within walking distance, and can you bring in your own caterer or private chef?

  6. Location and walkability

    A walkable venue gives participants free time without you organising transport for every outing. It also makes your marketing easier: beach, cafes and sunsets are part of the offer.

  7. Airport transfers

    Check the distance to Ngurah Rai Airport and plan group transfers. Traffic, not distance, decides how long it takes.

  8. Sleep quality

    Air-conditioning, blackout curtains and quiet at night matter more than anything on a retreat. Tired participants do not have transformative weeks.

  9. Wi-Fi and work space

    Even on a digital-detox retreat, you need to run your business. Ask for actual speeds.

  10. Security and staff

    Is there 24-hour security and a front desk? It reassures participants, especially women traveling alone.

  11. Daily housekeeping

    Clean rooms every day lift the whole experience and are one less thing for you to manage.

  12. What is included, in writing

    Get a clear list of what the venue provides and what you bring, so you can price your retreat with confidence.

Venue-only or all-inclusive?

All-inclusive retreat centers handle food, activities and sometimes even marketing. That is convenient, but you are fitting your retreat into their format. A venue-only property gives you the space and the hospitality, and leaves the programme entirely to you: your teachers, your food philosophy, your schedule and your price.

For independent facilitators building a recognisable retreat brand, venue-only is often the better fit.

Quick Answers

Good to Know

Check that you can have exclusive use of the property, that the room configuration fits your group and pricing, that there is a practice space with a covered backup for rain, how meals will work, how far the venue is from the airport, and what is and is not included.

A venue-only property gives you full control over your programme, teachers, food and pricing, and usually more character and privacy. An all-inclusive center does more for you but leaves less room to shape the experience. Many independent facilitators prefer venue-only.

Bali's wet season generally runs from around November to March, with heavy but often short tropical showers. If you plan a retreat in those months, make sure your venue has a covered space for practice.

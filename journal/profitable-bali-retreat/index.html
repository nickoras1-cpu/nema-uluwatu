How to Run a More Profitable Retreat in Bali

Off-season dates, school-holiday flight traps and partner discounts.

By the Nema. Uluwatu team · 23 October 2026 · 7 min read

Running a retreat in Bali is one of the most rewarding things a facilitator can do. It can also be one of the easiest ways to lose money, usually because of dates, not demand. Here is what we have learned from hosting groups at Nema.

In this guide
  1. Run it in the off-season
  2. Avoid the school-holiday flight trap
  3. A simple margin example
  4. Build margin into every line with partners
  5. Your date-setting checklist

1. Run it in the off-season

Accommodation is usually the largest fixed cost of a retreat. In Bali it is also the most seasonal. From November to March, rates across the island are at their lowest, while what your participants are willing to pay for a week of practice, rest and community in Bali stays much the same.

That gap is your margin. Price your retreat on the value of the experience, not on your costs, and the off-season lets you keep more of every booking, or offer a more generous package at the same price.

The off-season is Bali's wet season, but on the Bukit Peninsula rain usually comes in short afternoon bursts. Plan the main practice for the morning, keep a covered space for backup, and the weather rarely gets in the way. Our guide to the best time to visit Uluwatu has the month-by-month detail.

2. Avoid the school-holiday flight trap

This is the mistake we see most often. A retreat sells out months ahead, then participants start pricing flights and discover the dates sit in school holidays. Fares jump, and cancellations follow.

Before you fix your dates, check the school calendars for the countries most of your participants fly from, and price a few return flights from your main cities. Watch in particular for:

  • Australia: the long summer break from mid-December to late January, plus two-week breaks around April, July and late September to early October. Dates vary by state.
  • United Kingdom: Christmas, February half-term, Easter, late May half-term, the summer holidays from late July to early September, and October half-term.
  • Germany, the Netherlands and Switzerland: staggered summer holidays from July to September, plus Christmas, Easter and autumn breaks that vary by region.
  • Christmas and New Year: the busiest and most expensive time to fly to Bali from almost everywhere.

Combine that with Bali's off-season and the strongest windows are usually November, early December, February and March. In March, also check the date of Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, when the island and its airport close for 24 hours.

4. Build margin into every line with partners

Retreat margins are thin, so every line of the budget counts. A venue with good local partnerships lets you include more in your package while keeping more of the price. At Nema:

  • Partner restaurants, 10–20% off. Nema guests save 10–20% at partner restaurants such as YUKI, Kynd, Mood by Ours and Osteria Luna. Include a welcome dinner or closing feast in your package at the normal menu price, and the discount becomes your margin.
  • Villa catering can be arranged on request, keeping the group together and the schedule simple.
  • Fresh coconuts can be ordered in advance for your group: a low-cost, very Bali inclusion after morning practice that does not break the budget.
  • Transport partners for airport transfers and excursions, so you are not negotiating rides for a group of twelve.
  • A local itinerary. Using our local knowledge, we can help you design an agenda of local activities that works within your budget.
  • Scooter rental is available for participants who want to explore in their free time.
  • Drinks are available to buy on site, so you do not need to stock the property.
  • Seating and custom mats: partners can supply extra seating for gatherings, and our custom mat partner can create co-branded yoga mats for your retreat.

5. Your date-setting checklist

  1. List the three countries most of your participants will fly from.
  2. Rule out their school holidays, plus Christmas and New Year.
  3. Prefer November, early December, February or March, and check the Nyepi date.
  4. Price return flights from your main cities for your chosen week.
  5. Confirm the venue, then publish your dates six to nine months ahead.

Quick Answers

Good to Know

For the best margin, run your retreat in Bali's off-season, from November to March, when accommodation is at its lowest price, and choose weeks outside the main school holidays of the countries your participants fly from. In practice that often means November, early December, February and March.

A common reason is the cost of flights. When retreat dates fall in or near school holidays, airfares can rise sharply, and participants who booked the retreat early may cancel once they price their flights. Checking fares from your main origin cities before you fix dates helps avoid this.

Lower your biggest fixed cost by choosing off-season dates, keep your price based on the value of the experience, and avoid school-holiday flight spikes. Then use your venue's partners: at Nema, partner restaurants give guests 10–20% off, so you can include group meals at menu price and keep the difference, and transport, catering, coconuts and activities can all be arranged through Nema's local partners.

Nema works with many local partners: restaurants with 10–20% guest discounts, transport for airport transfers and excursions, villa catering, coconuts ordered in advance for the group, extra seating, custom co-branded yoga mats, and scooter rental. The team can also help design an itinerary of local activities to fit your budget, and drinks are available to buy on site.

Nema has 12 private studios with one queen bed each: 12 participants in private rooms, or up to 24 sharing. Classes run on the paved pool deck, which staff clear of furniture, or in the covered common area when it rains.

Uluwatu Without a Scooter: What You Can Walk To

Stay central, walk to the beach and the cafes, and save rides for the sunsets.

By the Nema. Uluwatu team · 23 October 2026 · 5 min read

Scooters are part of the Bali postcard, but they are not compulsory. If you stay in the right part of Uluwatu, you can spend a week here on foot, with the occasional app ride, and never miss a sunset. This is how.

Why skip the scooter?

Uluwatu's roads are steep, winding and busy, and some stretches are unlit at night. Indonesia also requires a valid licence that covers motorbikes, and many travel insurance policies will not cover you without one. For experienced riders, a scooter is freedom. For everyone else, it is the most common way a holiday goes wrong.

Stay central, not remote

Uluwatu is not one village; it is a collection of cliff-top areas spread across the Bukit Peninsula. Many beautiful villas are a long way from anything, which is wonderful until you want dinner. The single best decision for a scooter-free trip is to stay in central Pecatu, on or just off the main Uluwatu road, where cafes, spas and restaurants are on your doorstep.

Nema is right there: tucked down a short lane off the main road, so it is quiet inside but walkable outside.

When to take a ride

For anything beyond walking distance, you have three easy options:

  • Grab or Gojek. Bali's main ride-hailing apps, available as car or motorbike-taxi rides. You see the price and the driver before you book.
  • A local driver. In some areas, local transport rules limit where app drivers can pick up. If that happens, our front desk can help arrange a trusted local driver.
  • A day driver. For a day of temples and beaches, hiring a car and driver for the day is relaxed and good value.

Ngurah Rai International Airport is about 17 km away. Distance is not the issue; traffic is. Allow extra time, especially in the late afternoon.

A day in Uluwatu on foot

  1. 7:00 Walk the local pathway down to Thomas Beach for an early swim.
  2. 9:00 Brunch at Suka Espresso, three minutes from the gate.
  3. 11:00 Back to your studio's sunlit work nook, or straight to the pool.
  4. 16:00 A massage at Spring Spa Uluwatu, five minutes on foot.
  5. 18:00 A short ride to Single Fin for sunset, or the Kecak fire dance at Uluwatu Temple.
  6. 20:30 Dinner in central Pecatu, then a short walk home.

Yes, if you stay centrally. From central Pecatu you can walk to Thomas Beach, cafes, spas and restaurants, and use Grab, Gojek or a driver for longer trips to places like Uluwatu Temple, Bingin or the airport.

Grab and Gojek are the main ride-hailing apps in Bali and are widely used around Uluwatu. In some areas local transport rules limit where app drivers can pick up, so if a pickup is difficult, ask your accommodation to arrange a local driver.

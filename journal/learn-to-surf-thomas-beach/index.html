Learn to Surf at Thomas Beach, Uluwatu

Uluwatu's friendliest learner wave, a short walk from your studio.

By the Nema. Uluwatu team · 23 October 2026 · 7 min read

Uluwatu is famous for world-class reef breaks that most people should only watch from the cliffs. But a short walk from Nema, Thomas Beach has one of the area's friendliest waves for learning: softer, quieter and usually uncrowded. Here is how to learn to surf there, safely and without wasting a morning.

The wave at Thomas Beach

Thomas Beach sits on the Bukit Peninsula's west coast, just north of Padang Padang. It is a reef break with lefts and rights, and on small to medium swells, around head high and below, the waves are soft and playful with a relatively easy paddle out. It is a place to practise fundamentals rather than chase long rides, which is exactly what you want when you are learning.

It is also quiet. The walk down from the cliff, with steps at the end, keeps the crowds away, so you spend more time catching waves and less time dodging other surfers.

Because it breaks over reef rather than sand, Thomas Beach is not a place to teach yourself. Learn with an instructor who knows the currents and can choose the right tide.

When to go: season, tide and time of day

  • Season: the dry season, roughly April to October, brings south-east trade winds that blow offshore on Uluwatu's west-facing breaks, so waves are cleaner and more consistent. It is the best time to learn here.
  • Wet season: from November to March the wind is often onshore on this coast and currents can be stronger. Good instructors simply move beginners to the east side of the Bukit on those days.
  • Tide: mid to high tide. At low tide the reef is exposed and too shallow to surf safely.
  • Swell: small to medium. On bigger swells, a strong current can build from the sets breaking towards Uluwatu, and it is no longer a beginner wave.
  • Time of day: early mornings are usually the calmest and least crowded, and you are back for breakfast by the pool.

Reef safety and etiquette

  • Never surf alone as a beginner. Most Bali breaks have no lifeguards.
  • Wear reef booties if you are not confident, and fall flat and shallow rather than diving.
  • Respect the tide. If the reef is showing, the session is over.
  • Watch the current. If you feel it pulling, paddle across it, not against it, and signal your instructor.
  • Sun: a rash vest and reef-safe sunscreen; the equatorial sun is strong even on cloudy days.
  • Etiquette: do not drop in on someone already riding a wave, keep out of the way of surfers paddling back out, and be friendly to locals in the lineup.

A simple three-day beginner plan

  1. Day 1: a two-hour lesson on a soft-top board: paddling, popping up and reading the whitewater. Afternoon by the pool.
  2. Day 2: a second lesson at the best spot for the swell, aiming to catch green, unbroken waves with your instructor.
  3. Day 3: a supervised session or private lesson at Thomas Beach on a mid-to-high tide, then sunset at Single Fin to watch the experts on the main Uluwatu break.

Three mornings will not make you a surfer, but most people are standing and riding by the end of them, and hooked for life.

Surf for groups and retreats

Morning surf, afternoon yoga is one of the great Bali retreat formats, and Thomas Beach is on Nema's doorstep. For groups, we can help arrange lessons with local instructors, transport to other breaks when conditions change, and coconuts waiting when everyone gets back. See our retreat page or group stays.

Other beginner-friendly options nearby include Baby Padang (at mid to high tide) and Dreamland's sandy beach break, about 20 minutes away. Our guide to the best time to visit Uluwatu covers the seasons in more detail.

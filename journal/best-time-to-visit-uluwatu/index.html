The Best Time to Visit Uluwatu, Month by Month

Dry season, shoulder months, and the truth about the rain.

By the Nema. Uluwatu team · 23 October 2026 · 6 min read

Uluwatu has two seasons, dry and wet, and both are better than their reputation. The secret is that the best weeks are usually not the ones everyone books. Here is how the year really feels on the Bukit Peninsula.

The sweet spot: April to June, September to October

If you can choose your dates, choose these. The dry season brings sunshine and consistent surf, but the shoulder months either side of the July and August peak are when Uluwatu is at its best: beaches without crowds, easy restaurant bookings, and lower prices than July and August.

April is special. The rain has eased, the island is lush and green after the wet season, and outside Easter week most travellers have not arrived yet. At Nema our rates are seasonal, so the shoulder months are noticeably better value than the peak, while still under US$150 a night.

Peak season

July and August are the European summer holidays, Australia’s winter school break falls in July, and this is the busiest time of year across Bali. The weather is reliably beautiful, but beaches, day clubs and roads are at their fullest and prices are at their highest. August is our busiest month, so book early if these are your dates.

Flying in from Australia, the UK and Europe

  • From Australia: Perth is under four hours away and in the same time zone; the east coast is about six hours with almost no jet lag. Fares jump in the school holidays, around April, July, late September to early October and from mid-December. May, June and October give you dry-season weather at everyday prices.
  • From the UK: about 17 hours with one stop. Avoid Christmas, Easter and the late-July to early-September summer break. November, February and March are the quietest and cheapest months, and the rain here is gentler than its reputation.
  • From Germany, Switzerland and the Netherlands: 16 to 18 hours with one stop. Our guests from these countries tend to stay the longest, and a week or more makes the journey worthwhile. The shoulder months of April to June and September to October are ideal.

The truth about the rainy season

From November to March, Bali's wet season gets a bad name it does not entirely deserve, especially on the Bukit Peninsula, one of the drier parts of the island. Rain here usually arrives as a short, heavy downpour in the afternoon, and mornings are often bright and sunny.

  • It stays warm. The sea and the pool are perfect for swimming all year.
  • It is quiet. Fewer visitors, easier tables, emptier beaches.
  • It is the best value of the year, apart from the Christmas and New Year rush.
  • It is green. Gardens and bougainvillea are at their most lush.

At Nema, the compound is protected and calm in a storm. Swim in the warm pool, read on your balcony as the rain passes, and when it is time to move, practise under cover in the common area.

One date to check if you are coming in March: Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, when the whole island, airport included, pauses for 24 hours. It is a remarkable experience, but plan around it.
